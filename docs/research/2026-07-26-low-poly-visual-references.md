# New Model Motors: low-poly visual reference research

Date: 2026-07-26

## Question

How can New Model Motors use a low-poly automotive town without looking like a close imitation of Bruno Simon's portfolio?

## Conclusion

The strongest direction is **an editorial motor-show town built from matte architectural models, with glossy die-cast Model Vehicles as the only premium objects**.

Low polygon count is not the identity by itself. The identity should come from this contrast:

- The town is graphic, architectural, and almost diagrammatic.
- Vehicles are compact, reflective hero objects with strong silhouettes.
- Roads and destination landmarks do most of the wayfinding.
- Comedy comes from physical consequences and overly serious automotive rituals, not cartoon faces or joke copy everywhere.
- The scene stays bounded and deliberately sparse enough to work as an instant browser experience.

This combination draws different lessons from several references rather than inheriting one reference's complete look.

### Deliberate departures from Bruno Simon

The fixed high chase camera and direct driving are already shared territory, so the project must separate itself elsewhere:

- Build a **recognizable town of destinations**, not an abstract stunt playground.
- Make the visitor change from an Inspector Cart into one of several data-backed Model Vehicles; the vehicle is a model identity, not a single portfolio avatar.
- Use **matte architectural models versus glossy hero cars** as the signature material contrast.
- Make driving unlock benchmarks and editorial material rather than portfolio sections.
- Use deadpan dealership procedure and systemic object reactions for humor, not a collection of driving toys.
- Favor designed streets, dealership forecourts, and roadside institutions over a mostly open plane.

## References

### 1. art of rally — automotive silhouette and restrained scenery

Funselektor describes *art of rally* as a colorful, stylized, top-down racing experience, while its official gallery shows how a high camera, broad color fields, and compact vehicle silhouettes remain readable during motion. The studio also says the creator's travel, weather, and landscape experiences influenced the game's natural scenes and lighting. [Official game site](https://www.artofrally.com/) · [Funselektor studio history](https://www.funselektor.com/about)

**Borrow**

- Design each Model Vehicle from three or four unmistakable masses: cabin, hood, wheel blocks, and one model-specific accent.
- Use dark window shapes and a single dominant body color so vehicles read at the project's fixed high camera.
- Let lighting and terrain color establish a district's mood instead of adding surface textures everywhere.
- Reserve small, bright accents for headlights, brake lights, and interaction state.

**Do not copy**

- Do not use recognizable historic rally-car bodies, rally liveries, spectator stages, or the same hazy landscape language.
- Do not make the whole world equally soft and scenic. New Model Motors needs architectural destinations and navigable town logic.

### 2. Lonely Mountains: Downhill — readable routes inside large terrain shapes

Megagon presents *Lonely Mountains: Downhill* as a ride through an unspoiled landscape from peak to valley; its official materials emphasize trails, shortcuts, caves, waterfalls, and strongly differentiated environments. The studio's official page also highlights a selected review specifically praising its low-poly treatment of nature. [Official game page and gallery](https://lonelymountains.com/)

**Borrow**

- Compose Motor Town from a few large terrain masses first, then cut roads and landmarks through them.
- Signal side roads through material value, verge shape, and prop rhythm rather than floating arrows.
- Give each route one distant landmark that remains visible from the high camera: a dyno tower, drive-in screen, dealership roof, or sign.
- Cluster trees, lamps, barriers, and rocks at decision points; leave transit stretches quieter.

**Do not copy**

- Do not reproduce alpine terrain, downhill trail design, or nature as the main spectacle.
- Avoid hiding necessary destinations as secret shortcuts. Exploration can reward curiosity, but primary model content must remain findable.

### 3. Townscaper — modular architecture with irregular silhouettes

The official *Townscaper* site explains that colored blocks placed on an irregular grid are automatically converted into houses, arches, stairs, bridges, and backyards according to their configuration. That is a useful production model: variety comes from rules and adjacency, not hundreds of unique buildings. [Official project site](https://www.townscapergame.com/)

**Borrow**

- Build a small town kit: wall bays, corner bays, roof caps, arches, awnings, ramps, signs, and window modules.
- Give each Dealership a distinct assembly rule rather than a completely separate asset library.
- Use irregular footprints and roof heights to avoid the generic marketplace “low-poly city pack” look.
- Let doors, ramps, canopies, and signage emerge from building function so the town stays readable.

**Do not copy**

- Do not use Townscaper's coastal-European houses, candy palette, canals, or toy-building interaction.
- Do not add procedural generation merely because the modules permit it. Motor Town is a small authored place with intentional driving lines.

### 4. Mini Motorways — road-first wayfinding and disciplined color

Dinosaur Polo Club says *Mini Motorways* applies a minimalist approach to road-network design. Its first-party concept retrospective shows that road markings, parking-lot shapes, mountains, overpasses, and isometric presentation were repeatedly tested rather than treated as decoration. [Official game page](https://dinopoloclub.com/games/mini-motorways/) · [First-party concept retrospective](https://dinopoloclub.com/2023/07/23/behind-the-scenes-concepting-mini-motorways/)

**Borrow**

- Treat the road network as the main navigation UI.
- Assign every Content Destination a stable **shape + color + road-marking** combination, so color is never the only cue.
- Make entrances obvious in the ground plane: widened aprons, lane paint, curb shape, and a landmark silhouette.
- Keep the driving view nearly label-free; show text only when the vehicle slows near a destination.

**Do not copy**

- Do not reduce Motor Town to an infographic or reuse Mini Motorways' house/destination icon language.
- Avoid an all-pastel palette. The project needs stronger material hierarchy between matte town and glossy cars.

### 5. WHAT THE CAR? — the car itself can be the joke

Triband's creative director calls *WHAT THE CAR?* an absurd racing adventure filled with comedy. Apple's first-party launch description says its car repeatedly gains unexpected features such as legs, wings, and even a cold, with physics changing the play around those transformations. [Apple's launch article with the developer's description](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/05/apple-launches-20-fun-new-games-for-its-award-winning-apple-arcade-service/)

**Borrow**

- Let model identity alter one readable physical or visual behavior: wheelbase, suspension attitude, trail effect, horn, or body accessory.
- Use a normal automotive expectation as the setup, then break exactly one rule.
- Make the strange behavior immediately playable instead of explaining it through dialogue.

**Do not copy**

- Do not give cars limbs, wings, illnesses, or reproduce Triband's rapid succession of one-joke race levels.
- Do not let absurd transformations undermine the stable Model Vehicle silhouette needed for model comparison.

### 6. Untitled Goose Game — comedy through physical causality

House House's official premise is a peaceful village disrupted by a “horrible goose.” The joke is not a separate cutscene: the player directly manipulates ordinary village objects and produces the commotion. [Official game site and screenshots](https://goose.game/)

**Borrow**

- Put jokes in objects the vehicle can affect: a dealership's ceremonial rope collapses, a freshness meter spins too far, a directional sign reluctantly swivels back, or traffic cones become an accidental hood ornament.
- Use ordinary, serious settings as the straight man. The world should react more than it explains.
- Design a small number of reusable physical verbs—bump, push, drag, topple, trigger—and author comic setups around them.

**Do not copy**

- Do not add an animal mascot, English village, checklist structure, or NPC harassment loop.
- Do not make every prop dynamic. A few authored reactive objects will read better and cost less than universal destructibility.

### 7. Donut County — absurd systems presented plainly

The official description calls *Donut County* a story-filled physics puzzle in which the player is a hole that steals locals' trash and eventually the locals themselves. Its strength as a reference is the straight-faced presentation of one absurd physical rule. [Official project site](https://www.donutcounty.com/)

**Borrow**

- Give each destination one impossible-but-consistent automotive ritual: the Dyno Lab can treat benchmark bars as physical engine output; the Drive-In can project an article only after the correct car is parked.
- Escalate object behavior in two or three clear beats instead of firing many small jokes at once.
- Keep UI copy short and institutional, allowing the physical absurdity to carry the joke.

**Do not copy**

- Do not use the hole mechanic, animal townspeople, trash-collection loop, or its recognizable desert/suburban imagery.
- Do not turn every content interaction into a physics puzzle; the destination still has to deliver information efficiently.

### 8. Cloud Gardens — authored prop dioramas and visible age

Noio describes *Cloud Gardens* as small lo-fi dioramas of urban decay and manufactured landscapes, assembled from hundreds of item types and gradually overgrown by the player. This is a useful reference for making compact destinations feel specific without building a large world. [Official developer press kit](https://www.noio.nl/cloud-gardens-presskit/)

**Borrow**

- Treat the Dyno Lab, Drive-In, and each Dealership forecourt as a dense authored diorama inside a quieter town.
- Build prop families rather than one-offs: test equipment, cables, cones, paper cups, banners, lights, and obsolete model parts.
- Express Release Age through a controlled prop layer—dust outline, sun-faded placard, stacked recall notices—not by degrading the entire vehicle.

**Do not copy**

- Do not adopt post-apocalyptic ruins or plant overgrowth as the central freshness metaphor.
- Avoid uniform prop density. Dense clusters need empty space around them to remain legible from the high camera.

### 9. Slow Roads — a browser-first scene budget

*Slow Roads* is a WebGL driving experience hosted directly in the browser. In a first-person technical case study, its creator explains that smooth performance came from a pared-down design, corridor-based levels of detail, minimal physics, pooled geometry, compressed assets, and quality settings. The creator also reports that procedural generation kept initial scene generation to about 3.2 seconds on average and that more than 97% of measured users saw load times below ten seconds. [Playable official project](https://slowroads.io/) · [Creator-authored technical case study](https://web.dev/case-studies/slow-roads)

**Borrow**

- Treat performance constraints as art-direction constraints from the first graybox.
- Reuse geometry and materials aggressively; instance vegetation, lamps, barriers, and repeated building bays.
- Give dynamic physics only to objects that create a meaningful driving response or joke.
- Build explicit quality levels for shadows, reflections, foliage, antialiasing, and prop density.
- Begin scene initialization after a clear “Start engine” action so the loading transition belongs to the fiction.

**Do not copy**

- Do not build an infinite procedural road. Motor Town's identity depends on memorable authored places and repeat visits.
- Do not assume Slow Roads' narrow-corridor optimization transfers directly to a branching town. Use the fixed high camera and bounded map to establish a predictable visibility budget instead.

## Recommended visual system

### Geometry

- Use visibly faceted, low-poly geometry with clean silhouettes and no cartoon outlines.
- Make architecture from reusable bays; allow only destination landmarks and dealership entrances to use unique hero meshes.
- Keep vehicle wheels, cabin, lights, and model badge readable from the fixed camera before adding small detail.
- Use simple collision proxies separate from visible meshes.

### Materials

- **Town:** matte or lightly rough materials, broad color blocks, very limited texture maps.
- **Model Vehicles:** clearcoat-like body material, dark glass, sharper highlights.
- **Inspector Cart:** deliberately utilitarian—painted plastic or powder-coated metal—so entering a Model Vehicle feels like an upgrade.
- **Interactive props:** one consistent accent treatment, supported by motion or shape rather than glow everywhere.

The matte-town/glossy-car split is the most important differentiator from a generic low-poly driving portfolio.

### Color and lighting

- Choose one neutral town base and a small civic-signage palette.
- Give each Dealership a dominant color family, but preserve destination colors across the whole town.
- Use one authored daylight direction with strong, readable shadows; change local mood with emissive signage and interior light rather than a full lighting rewrite for every district.
- Check every route in grayscale and under color-vision-deficiency simulation because shape and value must carry navigation.

### Environmental comedy

- Prefer **setup → player contact → delayed consequence**.
- Let luxury-motoring ceremony collide with cheap town infrastructure.
- Keep jokes optional and discoverable; primary roads and content destinations must remain clear.
- Reuse a few comedic systems across locations so the scene does not require hundreds of bespoke animations.

### UI and wayfinding

- Keep the driving HUD to control hints, Active Flagship identity, and a lightweight destination cue.
- Put destination identity into architecture and ground markings before adding labels.
- Open detailed benchmark and article content as the existing 2D Model Dossier, not as tiny text inside the 3D world.
- Preserve vehicle position and orientation when the dossier opens and closes.

## Asset strategy

Do not begin by collecting a complete low-poly town pack. That tends to import someone else's proportions, palette, and visual identity.

Build or source in this order:

1. **Graybox road network and camera** — prove that driving, turning, and destination recognition work with primitives.
2. **Model Vehicle and Inspector Cart silhouettes** — validate scale and readability before materials.
3. **One modular Dealership kit** — wall, corner, roof, door, ramp, canopy, and sign; recolor and reassemble it for early company tests.
4. **Destination landmarks** — one unmistakable silhouette for each currently defined Content Destination.
5. **Shared prop families** — road furniture, dealership equipment, town clutter, and a small set of reactive comedy props.
6. **Lighting and material pass** — establish the matte/gloss hierarchy before adding asset density.

For any purchased or downloaded base asset, verify commercial-use rights, editable source files, texture sizes, triangle count, rigging, and whether redistribution in a web bundle is allowed. Normalize selected assets to the project's own proportions, palette, materials, and collision rules; do not mix marketplace packs unchanged.

## First prototype to answer the art-direction question

Create one small intersection containing:

- the Inspector Cart;
- one Model Vehicle;
- one Dealership entrance;
- one destination landmark;
- one quiet road;
- one reactive comedy prop;
- the fixed high camera.

Test only three questions:

1. Can a new visitor identify where to drive without a floating arrow?
2. Does the Model Vehicle feel materially special against the town?
3. Does bumping the comedy prop feel like part of this world rather than an unrelated gag?

If these work, the style is sufficiently distinct to scale into Motor Town. If they do not, adding more low-poly assets will only hide the unresolved art direction.
